BidetFit/Skirted toilets
Plumbing problem

The seat may fit while the adapter does not.

A skirted toilet hides the trapway and may also hide the underside tank connection where many bidet kits expect a T-adapter.

Run two separate checks

CheckQuestionEvidence
Seat geometryCan the mounting plate and housing sit flat?Bolt spacing, rear clearance, bowl length, photos
Water connectionCan the supplied adapter connect to an accessible fitting?Photo of shutoff, hose, tank inlet, product installation diagram

Passing one check does not imply passing the other. This is a common weakness in compatibility lists that classify only by bowl shape.

Look for the actual connection path

Trace the flexible supply line from the wall shutoff. On a conventional toilet, it usually reaches an exposed fill-valve connection beneath the tank. On some skirted designs, the line or connection disappears behind porcelain or an access panel. Do not assume there is enough hand or tool clearance just because part of the hose is visible.

Possible product-specific solutions

Some manufacturers sell or approve alternate adapters at the wall shutoff or another accessible point. Whether that is permitted depends on the product, fitting sizes, local plumbing rules, and the exact toilet. Do not substitute random fittings or stack adapters based only on thread appearance.

Before ordering: send the merchant a clear plumbing photo and ask where the supplied adapter installs on your exact toilet. Save the written answer.

Wall-hung is a different category

A wall-hung toilet with an in-wall tank is not merely “very skirted.” The water path and seat-fixing hardware can be fundamentally different. Use the exact toilet and bidet installation manuals and expect professional confirmation.
