BidetFit/Electric vs. non-electric
Category decision

Choose by constraint before feature.

Warm water and drying are irrelevant if the housing hits the tank or there is no suitable outlet. Start with what the bathroom can support.

CategoryTypical strengthsMain compatibility gates
Electric replacement seatHeated seat, controlled water temperature, drying, remote or side controlsExact bowl size, rear clearance, bolt spacing, French curve, flat deck, suitable power
Non-electric attachmentLower cost, no outlet, keeps current seatRear geometry, bracket range, seat-bumper gap, accessible water connection

When electric makes sense

The toilet passes the dimensional screen, a suitable receptacle is in the product's permitted location, and the buyer values temperature control or drying enough to accept higher cost and more components. Confirm cord side and length; “an outlet exists” does not mean it is reachable.

When non-electric is the rational choice

There is no outlet, the buyer wants a simpler installation, or cost matters more than heated features. Non-electric does not mean universal: attachments still need a flat-enough rear deck, bracket range, and water access.

When handheld deserves a look

A French-curve or unusual bowl blocks under-seat hardware, but the plumbing remains accessible. The tradeoff moves from seat geometry to hose control, mounting, spray handling, and shutoff discipline.

Safety boundary: BidetFit does not recommend permanent bathroom extension-cord use or improvised plumbing fittings. Use the product instructions and qualified local help where required.
