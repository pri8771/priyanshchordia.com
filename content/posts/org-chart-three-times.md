*Each change was one sentence. Each one is on the record, with a way back.*

I build automation and AI agent systems, and I run a company where AI bots do the work. One morning I asked my chief-of-staff bot, SuperBot, for a plan for the three bots that handle jobs, the shop and infrastructure, and then a plan for the next layer of worker bots. Then I changed what I wanted.

Here is the first change, word for word, from decision 64 in my decisions log:

> instead make the three worker bots exact copies of Superbot, but in their soul file write that they report to Superbot. And Superbot reports to me.

So the three bots had their settings, soul file and memory notes overwritten with SuperBot's, plus one added line saying who they report to. The plan that was already running was stopped as out of date.

The second change is decision 65, and it is a correction of the first:

> No, I didn't mean jobs and shop and infra are exact copies of Superbot. I meant the three engineering bots would be.

The three bots were restored from backup, byte for byte, and six new engineering bots were cloned: three on a hosted model, three on a free local one. The third change is decision 66:

> this is getting a little too complicated. One more update. Make all the bots just exact copies of Superbot.

When that was done, the number of skill differences between the six and SuperBot was zero.

![the counter, 15 of 45 then 23 of 79](/blog-media/org-chart-three-times/decision-count.gif)

The technical part is the reason none of this was a problem. Every instruction I give is stored as a numbered decision with three fields: my exact words, a Reading of what was understood, and a Done field of what changed. For the overwrite, the old soul and settings were already in a backup folder, and the Done field gave its path. Undoing my mistake was a copy, not an investigation.

I also asked for a script to count how often I do this. It looks for phrases like "change of plans" and "corrects", so the count is rough. At the point I wrote this it flagged 23 of 79 decisions as changing something already decided, and earlier the same day, 15 of 45. I can fit several changes into one sentence, so a decision is the unit it counts, and it undercounts me:

> Number two, no, actually add on to number one. Do as he asks. No, change it to do as he says.

![decision 76, as dictated](/blog-media/org-chart-three-times/number-two.gif)

Three bots restored, six built, zero differences, one log.
