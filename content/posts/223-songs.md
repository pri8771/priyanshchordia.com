My audio folder has the songs. My rights inventory has a job for me.

On Nov 23, I said, “I also think it would be really cool if we could create our own AI music albums and release it on Spotify.” That’s in my album plan. The part about creating the music was already fairly well along.

My app’s audio folder holds 392 MP3s, about 274 MB. Of those, 223 are named for Suno, the AI music tool. They take up about 248 MB and the tracks typically run 1 to 3 minutes. I can tell you how much space the music takes up. I have 0 files with a per-track record of which plan made them.

![The folder count narrows from 392 MP3s to 223 Suno files to 0 with a per-track plan record](/blog-media/223-songs/audio-folder.gif)

I keep a rights inventory. In June 2026 it called the meditation, energy and smiley tracks “AI-generated original” and marked all 1,166 rows “owner review needed”. I’m the owner. I also gave myself a way to narrow it down. The inventory flags 167 rows as high risk.

Here’s what a review involves. Under the Sept 2026 terms I checked that day, commercial use is allowed for songs made and downloaded while on a paid plan. Free-plan songs stay non-commercial after you subscribe, although Suno may grant rights case by case. Its help text and its terms disagree about songs made before subscribing. So the record I need for each track has to cover the plan when I made it and the date I downloaded it. The MP3 can play perfectly well without either detail.

The only generation record I found was a 3 KB log from one run in June. It doesn’t say which plan was active. It doesn’t give a download date per track. That leaves me with a folder I can count and an inventory I can review, and for any particular song I still have to go back to the library.

Spotify allows AI music. It wants AI credits through the distributor and it penalizes impersonation and spam. RouteNote, DistroKid, Amuse and Ditto accept AI music. Getting a distributor to accept the file still leaves the lyrics. Releasing a track requires exclusive rights to 100% of the material, including the words. Third-party lyrics would take a track out of consideration.

My album plan starts with 8 to 12 tracks with original lyrics. For each one I’ll check Suno’s library for the plan that was active at the time. Anything that fails gets regenerated under a plan that allows commercial use. The album name will go under a labelled brand. The distributor’s free tier comes after the library check.

![The release plan moves from original lyrics to a library check and then to regeneration for tracks that fail](/blog-media/223-songs/album-plan.gif)

That’s where the album stands as of Nov 23. The next thing I’ll do is pick the tracks with original lyrics and look up their records in Suno.
