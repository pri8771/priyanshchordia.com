DRAFT — a working copy; it may change.

2026-11-15 / Running a company on AI agents / DRAFT / 2 min read

I asked for bots in trios. Then I had to track them.

DRAFT. Not yet published. This page is a working copy for review and is not indexed by search engines.

I kept one lead per team and archived the extra profiles.

That morning I was asking for bots in trios. Later that day I asked for fewer bots to keep track of. The extra profiles went into an archive, which kept the change reversible. Independent checkers went through the config files that day to see which arrangement I actually had.

The trios came from my own instructions. I’d asked for a group to run experiments, and a group to do research, and a group for startup work. The fleet reflected the way I’d described the teams. Then I gave a more specific instruction about the part of the arrangement that involved me.

From my Nov 23 message, recorded in section 124 of the owner decisions file:

Also, I want a minimal number of bots, and I would prefer a maximum number of sub-agents, just so I can communicate to the bots one-on-one and I don't have to track 50 of them. So even if I say three engineering, three this, three that, maybe it's one with 9 sub-agents. I don't know. Propose something.

A sub-agent is a worker a lead can give a piece of a job to. Take the research team. Under the proposal I’d talk to Research about the work I wanted done. Research would decide what to hand off. Reading and drafting could go to workers running on the free local model, and the lead would make decisions on the strongest hosted model. From my end it would still be a conversation with Research.

In my message the number of workers is left open: "I don't know. Propose something." The proposal named the leads: SuperBot, Builder, Ideas, Money, Research, Startup, Media and one more, with a personal one later.

A proposal gives you a list of names. The independent checkers had something more specific to look at that day. Each lead had a soul file, a written description of who the bot is. Each profile also had a config file with a section for handing off work and an address for the local model. The checkers found that section and that address in every lead’s config.

The check came back with 9 leads. That was the number of leads across the teams. The workers in my engineering example had been a suggestion about what could sit under a lead.

The checkers also looked for the profiles I’d taken out of the lineup. Archiving them made the consolidation reversible, and it gave the checkers somewhere to look for the old setup. They found 10 archived profiles in a folder with a README, plus two backup folders.
